Text and Network Visualization with ArXiv Papers¶

Peter Silverstein¶

This assignment for the QMSS Data Visualization course at Columbia University focused on text and network analysis and visualization. The dataset is a set of ArXiv papers.

Text Analysis and Visualization¶

Term Frequency Analysis¶

For this chart, I chose a very simple horizontal bar chart for ease of readability and comparison between terms. Interestingly, "Network" was the most common term by far, with a steep drop-off to the second most common term, "de", and a somewhat steady decline after that.

This visualization takes the most common terms over the 2019 to 2023 period and then looks at their relative frequency over each year. The result is a visualization that shows variation over time. Interestingly, many of the terms seem to get more popular over time, indicating that they are on the upswing.

Term Co-occurence¶

I chose to both weight edges and color nodes by weights, so a dark red node indicates high usage of the term, and a thick edge indicates high co-usage. Interestingly, you can see that the word "de" is highly used (dark red) but has far fewer thick edges stemming from it than "model", which is comparatively less used but is used with other top terms more often.

Network Visualization¶

Per the instructions in the assignment, I did include labels for the network, though I think they make the visualization less helpful, especially given that the interactivity allows users to explore names for themselves. I do enjoy the juxtaposition of node color (centrality) and node size (number of author publications), because it highlights some authors with high publication numbers who don't have a lot of co-authorship as well as the opposite. Or, you're like Federico Battiston and you publish and co-author a lot of papers.

For both network visualizations, I pretty much think that they are useless without the interactivity, at least on this small-screen scale. The interactivity allows for exploration and isolation of individual nodes for observation in a way that a static image would not.
